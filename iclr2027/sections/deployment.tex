\section{Deploying the finding: decision-aware compression}
\label{sec:deployment}

Section~\ref{sec:realization} ended on a constructive statement: transcripts realize replay \emph{if} their decision content survives. We now test whether that statement can be turned into a compression \emph{policy} that beats the naive baseline an engineer would actually ship: top-truncation to the token cap.

\subsection{A paired-budget compression test}
\label{ssec:dctest}

The policy (\textsc{dslice}) is deterministic---no LLM, no paraphrase, no generated natural language and no new semantic values. The \emph{base card} (rules D1--D5) retains one canonical copy of each distinct action (exact-duplicate non-mutating actions are collapsed); compresses each read/aggregate tool result to a one-line summary (table, filter, matched-row count, retrieval-key columns of the first matched row); drops list-table dumps and write-operation results; and keeps the finish step and its result. Cards still exceeding 300 tokens escalate deletion in a fixed order: of 640 cards, 14 fit at the base stage and the rest delete successively read-summary key columns (76 cards), read result lines (281), the finish \emph{result} (8), non-decision action lines (252), and aggregate action lines (9). What is retained at 100\% by assertion is therefore narrower than the base card: parsed write (insert/update/delete) action arguments, every aggregate value, and the finish \emph{action} ($0$ QA failures over 640 cards; construction audit in Figure~\ref{fig:hdccards}). Cards average 237.5 tokens (range 102--299).

The control is the engineer's baseline done fairly: the \emph{same} full transcript, top-truncated not to 300 tokens but to \emph{exactly the token count of the paired \textsc{dslice} card} (\textsc{raw\_matched}; per-card $|\Delta\text{tokens}|=0$ for 640/640 pairs). Length can therefore not confound the comparison in either direction. One property of the source deserves emphasis: both card types inherit the provenance of the H-C raw harvest, and for 188 of the 640 memories (29.4\%; 52/49/43/44 in A00/A01/A10/A11) no harvest attempt succeeded, so the stored trajectory is a deterministic reconstruction of the evaluator's oracle plan rather than a model-generated episode. All arms consume the identical 640 sources, so pairing and budget exactness are unaffected; but the pooled contrast mixes two provenance strata, which we stratify post-hoc in \S\ref{ssec:dcstrength}. We also report the ecological 300-token cut (\textsc{raw(300)}) as the deployment senior baseline. A timestamped pre-analysis protocol and its amendments (including the addition of the 3B arm and the paired-budget control; registration provenance disclosed in Appendix~\ref{app:hdc}) govern the design; $12{,}800$ new rollouts cover the full grid $\{40$ families $\times$ 4 siblings $\times$ 4 cells $\times$ 4 seeds $\times$ 2 models$\}$ for \textsc{dslice} and \textsc{raw\_matched}, plus the missing \textsc{raw(300)} 3B arm.

Table~\ref{tab:hdc} (rates and CIs visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:hdc}) is the pooled evidence. On 7B, naive truncation at \emph{exactly} the same per-card budget realizes no replay at all ($\taureplay\approx-0.03$), while \textsc{dslice} realizes $+0.112$; the paired gap E1 $=+14.5$pp is Holm-significant. The guardrails behave as required: HFR is, if anything, \emph{lower} under \textsc{dslice} on both models (noninferiority established at the $+5$pp margin), and at 7B the pair shows a secondary $\tautrap$ shift of $+0.139$ in the protective direction (lower 95 $+0.075$). At 3B $\tautrap$ is unchanged on average ($+0.000$) and the $-5$pp noninferiority bound is not established (lower 95 $-0.072$)---we report this rather than pass it. At 3B we otherwise find no pooled evidence of benefit: the E1 point estimate is $-8.4$pp against \textsc{raw\_matched} (the CI $[-0.177,+0.002]$ excludes neither a moderate harm nor zero), and the \textsc{raw(300)} comparison is negative (secondary, unadjusted CI $-0.123$ [$-0.216,-0.039$]). A post-hoc cross-model difference of E1 ($+0.230$ [$+0.131,+0.338$], outside the Holm family) supports a pooled effect that differs by scale; it speaks to the size of the pooled effect, not to a capability mechanism---and it is subject to the provenance caveat of \S\ref{ssec:dcstrength}, which applies to both scales.

\subsection{Strength of evidence}
\label{ssec:dcstrength}

\paragraph{What the pooled result licenses.} A \emph{packaged} deterministic policy derived from the coverage finding beats naive truncation at an exactly paired budget on the larger model, with the primary harmful-flip guardrail intact at both scales and the secondary trap-shift guardrail established at 7B. We do not claim that any single rule inside the package (action retention, result summarization, escalation order) is responsible, and \textsc{dslice} does not reach procedural-card realization (A11 $.670$ vs.\ $.733$): decision-aware compression of a transcript closes much of the realization gap but does not close it.

\paragraph{What it does not license.} A post-hoc provenance stratification (the 188/640 oracle-reconstructed sources of \S\ref{ssec:dctest}; family-cluster bootstrap over the same resample semantics as the primary analysis; outside the Holm family; numbers in Appendix~\ref{app:hdc}) materially qualifies the interpretation. On authentic model-generated trajectories the 7B advantage is $+0.041$ [$-0.078,+0.149$], while on oracle-reconstructed trajectories it is $+0.424$ [$+0.255,+0.615$]; at 3B the strata give $-0.226$ [$-0.347,-0.104$] and $+0.297$ [$+0.167,+0.473$]. A direct provenance interaction (one aligned family-cluster bootstrap over the union family universe) converts this from two separate stratum impressions into one heterogeneity estimate: oracle-minus-authentic E1 is $+0.382$ [$+0.166,+0.606$] at 7B and $+0.523$ [$+0.306,+0.783$] at 3B. We take no position on the mechanism: the \textsc{dslice} replay term is nearly identical across strata ($+0.117$ authentic vs.\ $+0.104$ oracle at 7B) while the \textsc{raw\_matched} control swings ($+0.076$ vs.\ $-0.319$)---the strata differ mostly in how much the naive baseline loses, for reasons this design does not identify. We therefore state the deployment claim precisely: \textsc{dslice}-style compression beats exact-budget truncation as a \emph{benchmark} contrast on this mixed-provenance grid, with no pooled evidence of benefit at 3B (point estimate $-8.4$pp); whether the policy helps when the stored episodes are genuinely model-written remains open, and answering it requires a fallback-free re-harvest (new rollouts) that we flag as the blocking follow-up experiment rather than assume away. The pre-analysis amendments that make the pooled comparison fair (paired-budget control, 3B completion, tokenization no-op verification) are disclosed in Appendix~\ref{app:hdc}.
